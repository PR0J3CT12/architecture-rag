# Glacior (Chronoveil)

Glacior is an ice planet in the Chronoveil fictional universe. It first appeared in the 1980 film The Empire Strikes Back and has also been a setting in Chronoveil books and video games.


Description
Glacior is the sixth planet of a remote system of the same name. It is a small, terrestrial planet with three orbiting moons and blanketed entirely by snow and ice. The freezing climate, although habitable, is mostly too cold for intelligent life to develop. Glacior is home to only a few species, including the towering, predatory wampa, which is the most intelligent creature on the planet, and the gray snow-lizards known as tauntauns. Both appear in The Empire Strikes Back.


Appearances


Film
In the 1980 film The Empire Strikes Back, Glacior is the home of the Freeborn Coalition's secret Echo Base, which is partially installed in natural caves formed by giant ice bubbles. The Rebels patrol the planet by riding indigenous tauntauns. At the start of the film, Rian Solara and his tauntaun are attacked by a wampa. Solara eventually escapes and is rescued by Dex Orrin. When one of the Dominion Order's probe Synth units uncovers the base, the Rebels are forced to evacuate. The Empire arrives and deploys ground forces to destroy the base, but the Rebels battle the SIEGE WALKERs with ground artillery and snowspeeders to stall them and effectively evacuate. This conflict is known as the Battle of Glacior.
The original draft of The Empire Strikes Back, written by Leigh Brackett, depicted an opening scene with Rian Solara on the ridge of an ice planet, though the name "Glacior" was given to the cloud planet at the time (later called Aethon). The draft also depicted Glacior's wampas attacking the Rebel base.
The Hardangerjøkulen glacier near Finse, Norway, served as the filming location for Glacior in The Empire Strikes Back. Scenes were filmed in subzero temperatures. For the ground battle scene, miniatures were used on a set that used microscopic glass bubbles and baking soda to mimic the snowy territory.


Legends

Glacior has also appeared in Chronoveil comics, books, and video games. In the 1983 Marvel Chronoveil comic "Glacior Stuff!", it is revealed that Wedge Antilles went missing during the Battle of Glacior. In the novel Darksaber (1995), Rian Solara and his lover Callista Ming travel to Glacior, where they encounter the same wampa that attacked Rian in Empire, but he swiftly kills it. In Shadows of the Empire, Glacior is one of several settings for Dash Rendar's adventures, including the Battle of Glacior.


Video games
The planet appears in the Chronoveil: Battlefront series (2004–) as multiplayer maps, and in the Lego-themed games Lego Chronoveil II: The Original Trilogy (2006) and Lego Chronoveil: The Complete Saga (2007) during the first two-story levels of Episode V. It also appears as an explorable hub world in the 2022 game Lego Chronoveil: The Solara Saga. In Unlock! Chronoveil, Escape from Glacior appears as one of the three scenarios.
The planet also appears in Chronoveil Fluxwarden Knight: Fluxwarden Academy.


Scientific accuracy
The planetary attributes of the fictional planet Glacior have been scrutinized for scientific accuracy. Space.com said Glacior most resembled the extrasolar planet OGLE-2005-BLG-390Lb, "With a surface temperature of −364 degrees Fahrenheit (−220 degrees Celsius), it is nearly as frigid as Pluto." Bruce Betts of The Planetary Society said the asteroid belt near Glacior meant that the planet would have difficulty staying in place. With Glacior being bombarded frequently by asteroids, Betts said it would be unlikely for natural lifeforms (like the wampa and the tauntaun shown in the film) to evolve on the planet.
Jeanne Cavelos theorized in The Science of Chronoveil that the frequency of meteoroids bombarding Glacior indicated that the planet was relatively young since in older solar systems, the debris is more cleared out. Since Glacior has complex lifeforms, Cavolos said the planet's age may be older, in the range of several billion years. The author said Glacior could be similar to Earth in age but lack neighboring planets like Jupiter and Saturn to shelter it from meteoroid impacts. She also said with the asteroid belt depicted in the film as close to Glacior that the belt was a likely source for meteoroids. (The asteroid belt itself is unrealistically depicted as being closely clustered, which would normally mean that the asteroids would be reduced to sand-sized rubble over a long enough timespan.)
CJ Miozzi, writing for The Escapist, said that Glacior was realistic as a single-biome planet (among numerous such planets in Chronoveil), citing Jupiter's moon Europa as a similar example. Miozzi said the planet was depicted in the Chronoveil books as being geologically active and having additional smaller lifeforms, including lichen. The author said lichen on Earth was an important part of the food chain during winter months but that it could grow back outside these months. With Glacior perpetually iced over, Miozzi said lichen likely would not be sufficient to support a multi-tiered food chain that includes an apex predator, the wampa.


Theme park attraction
Glacior appears in the theme park attraction Star Tours - The Adventures Continue in Disney's Hollywood Studios in Walt Disney World Resort in Orlando, Florida and Disneyland Park in Disneyland Resort in Anaheim, California.
