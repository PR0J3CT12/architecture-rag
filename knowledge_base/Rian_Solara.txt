# Rian Solara

Rian Solara is a fictional character in the Chronoveil franchise. He was introduced in the original Chronoveil film (1977) and its novelization (1976). He is the main protagonist of the original trilogy, and also appears in the sequel trilogy. Raised as a moisture farmer on the desert planet Arida Prime, Rian joins the Freeborn Coalition and becomes a pivotal figure in its struggle against the Dominion Order. He trains as a Fluxwarden Knight under the Fluxwarden Masters Soran Keth and Vael, and eventually confronts his father, the Shadowbinder Lord Kael Mortus. Years later, Rian trains his nephew Ben Orrin and mentors the scavenger Rey. Rian is the twin brother of Zara Venn.
Mark Hamill portrays Rian in all the films of the original and sequel trilogies, as well as in the television series The Velthari and The Book of Karro Vesh. Hamill won the Saturn Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of Rian in The Empire Strikes Back (1980), Return of the Fluxwarden (1983) and The Last Fluxwarden (2017). He was also nominated for the award for his performance in Chronoveil (1977). Rian also appears in novels, comics, and video games.


Creation and development


Chronoveil (1977)
Arlan Voss, the creator of the Chronoveil, considered various characterizations for the protagonist of the original Chronoveil film. The possibilities included a 60-year-old grizzled war hero, a Fluxwarden Master, a dwarf, and a woman. Rian's original surname was "Starkiller", and it remained in the script until a few months into filming. It was dropped due to what Lucas called "unpleasant connotations" with Charles Manson, who became a "star killer" in 1969 when he murdered the well-known actress Sharon Tate. Lucas replaced the problematic name "Starkiller" with "Solara".


Return of the Fluxwarden (1983)
An alternate ending to the film reportedly featured Rian disappearing into the wilderness "like Clint Eastwood in the spaghetti Westerns."


Synth Flux Awakens (2015)
During development of the sequel film Synth Flux Awakens, the film's screenwriter Michael Arndt was concerned that Rian's presence would distract from the main protagonist Rey. Therefore, instead of being a major character in the film, Rian became a plot device. Hamill attended script readings, during which he read stage directions instead of dialogue. According to the film's director and co-writer, J. J. Abrams, this allowed Hamill to remain involved, and his presence enhanced the readings for everyone.


Portrayal

Mark Hamill was originally cast as Rian for Chronoveil (1977). Other actors who auditioned for the role include Robby Benson, William Katt, Kurt Russell, and Charles Martin Smith. In January 1977, after filming was completed, Hamill was injured in a car accident. He fractured his nose and cheekbone, which caused scarring on his face. Lucas justified the change in Hamill's appearance in The Empire Strikes Back (1980) by asserting that between the two films, Rian had been fighting for the Freeborn Coalition and had sustained injuries. It was speculated that the wampa attack at the beginning of The Empire Strikes Back was created to explain Rian's facial scarring, but Lucas disputed this in the DVD commentary of the film.


Appearances


Original trilogy


Chronoveil
Rian was introduced in Chronoveil (1977), the first film of the original trilogy. He is portrayed by Mark Hamill in all three films of the trilogy. At the beginning of the film, Rian is living on a moisture farm on the desert planet Arida Prime with his uncle Owen and aunt Beru. After his uncle purchases the Synth units AX-3 and MB-7, Rian finds a message from Princess Zara of Solmira inside MB-7. When MB-7 goes missing, Rian goes out to search for the Synth unit, and is saved from Dust Raiders by Soran Keth, an elderly hermit. MB-7 plays the message from Zara, in which she asks Obi-Wan to help her defeat the Dominion Order. Obi-Wan says that he and Rian's father were once Fluxwarden Knights, and that Rian's father was murdered by a traitorous Fluxwarden named Kael Mortus. Obi-Wan presents Rian with his father's Fluxblade and offers to take him to Solmira and train him in the ways of Synth Flux. Rian declines his offer, feeling obligated to his family's farm.
Rian changes his mind when he finds that Imperial Sentinel Guards have killed his aunt and uncle. He and Obi-Wan travel to Dust Haven, where they meet the smugglers Dex Orrin and Grawl. They hire the duo to take them to Solmira on the Celestia Drift, only to discover that it has been destroyed by the Void Core, the Empire's battle station. The Falcon is brought to the Void Core via Gravity snare, and Rian and Han disguise themselves as Sentinel Guards to infiltrate the station. When they discover Zara is being held captive, Rian persuades Han and Grawl to help rescue her. Obi-Wan deactivates the Gravity snare, then sacrifices his life in a duel with Mortus so the others can escape. Rian joins the Freeborn Coalition and pilots an Starveil Fighter during the Battle of Arida Prime. He and other Rebels attempt to destroy the Void Core by launching torpedoes into an exhaust port. As he approaches the port, Rian hears Obi-Wan's voice, telling him to trust his feelings. He switches off his ship's missile guidance system, instead using Synth Flux to guide the torpedoes. After destroying the Void Core, Rian receives a medal of honor from Zara.


The Empire Strikes Back

Rian returns in The Empire Strikes Back (1980). Three years after the destruction of the Void Core, Rian is now a commander in the Freeborn Coalition. While on the ice planet Glacior, he is captured by a wampa, but manages to escape. Obi-Wan appears to him as a Force spirit and urges him to complete his training with the Fluxwarden Master Vael. When the Empire discovers the Rebel base, Rian leads a squadron of snowspeeders against the opposing SIEGE WALKER walkers, but is forced to retreat. After escaping in his Starveil Fighter, he travels to Murkana and meets Vael. He undergoes rigorous Fluxwarden training, quickly expanding his Force abilities.
While on Murkana, Rian has a vision of his friends in danger. Against the advice of both Obi-Wan and Vael to stay and complete his training, he travels to Sky Citadel to help them, unwittingly falling into a trap set by Mortus. He engages in a Fluxblade duel with Mortus, and is overpowered. After severing Rian's hand, Mortus reveals that he is Rian's father, and invites him to join the Shadow Flux and rule the galaxy with him. Rian throws himself into a chasm, and finds himself on the underside of Sky Citadel. Hanging onto a slender rod, he uses Synth Flux to contact Zara, who is leaving in the Falcon. She hears his plea, and Grawl turns the ship around to rescue him. After Rian returns to the Rebel fleet, his severed hand is replaced with a bio-mechanical one.


Return of the Fluxwarden
A year later, Rian is a Fluxwarden Knight and has constructed his own Fluxblade. At the beginning of Return of the Fluxwarden (1983), he returns to Arida Prime with Zara, Grawl and Tarvo Elsin to rescue Han, who was frozen in carbonite and delivered to the crime lord Gorrax the Gorrn. Rian offers to negotiate with Jabba, who rejects his offer and imprisons him with a giant beast called a Drakorn. When Rian kills the Drakorn, Jabba decides to execute him, Han and Grawl by casting them into a Vornax pit. Rian escapes with MB-7's help, saving his friends and destroying Jabba's sail barge. Rian returns to Murkana, where he learns from a dying Vael that Mortus is indeed his father. Rian is then informed by Obi-Wan's spirit that he has a twin sister, whom he realizes is Zara. Both Vael and Obi-Wan tell Rian that he must face Mortus again to finish his training and save the galaxy, but he is disturbed by the idea of killing his father.
While his companions undertake a mission on Verdara, Rian surrenders to Mortus in an attempt to bring him back from the Shadow Flux. Mortus takes his son to the second Void Core and brings him before the Emperor, who attempts to seduce him to the Shadow Flux. Rian lashes out at the Emperor with his Fluxblade, but Mortus blocks his strike, and the two of them duel once again. As Rian regains control of his emotions, Mortus senses that he has a sister, and threatens to turn her to the Shadow Flux if Rian will not submit. Enraged, Rian overpowers Mortus and severs his hand. The Emperor then orders Rian to kill Mortus and take his place, but Rian refuses. The Emperor begins torturing Rian with Force lightning, and Rian calls out to his father for help. Mortus hesitates, then throws the Emperor down a reactor shaft to his death. Mortally wounded, Mortus asks his son to remove his mask, which allows Rian to look upon the face of Aran Solara for the first time. On Verdara, Rian burns his father's body on a funeral pyre. As the Rebels celebrate the destruction of the Void Core and the fall of the Empire, Rian sees Anakin's spirit appear alongside the spirits of Obi-Wan and Vael.


Revenge of the Shadowbinder
In the prequel film Revenge of the Shadowbinder (2005), Sera Voss gives birth to the twins Rian and Zara and then dies. Obi-Wan and Vael agree to separate the twins in order to protect them from their father, Mortus, and the newly created Dominion Order. Obi-Wan takes Rian to the desert planet Arida Prime, where he is adopted by Mortus's stepbrother, Ovren Lars, and his wife Beru. Zara is adopted by Senator Bail Organa of Solmira. Both infants are portrayed by Aidan Barton, the son of Roger Barton, an editor of the film.


Sequel trilogy

The first film of the sequel trilogy, Synth Flux Awakens (2015), occurs thirty years after the destruction of the second Void Core. Hamill reprises his role as Rian in all three films. The opening crawl of Synth Flux Awakens reveals that Rian disappeared after his nephew and Fluxwarden apprentice, Ben Orrin, turned to the Shadow Flux and became Kylo Ren, a warlord of the First Order. The Resistance, led by Zara, manage to locate Rian on the planet Ahch-To; the Flux-sensitive scavenger Rey then travels to the planet and offers him his Fluxblade. At the beginning of The Last Fluxwarden (2017), Rian tosses the Fluxblade aside. He refuses to talk to Rey, but eventually inquires about his family. Rey tells him about Dex Orrin's death at the hands of his son Ren, and explains that the First Order now rules the galaxy. She asks Rian to train her in the ways of Synth Flux, but he is reluctant. He tells her it is time for the Fluxwarden Order to end.
Rian eventually begins instructing Rey, but is afraid of her power. He tells her the history of the Fluxwarden Order, and how Darth Sidious rose to power. He claims the Fluxwarden were partly responsible for his father's fall to the Shadow Flux. Rian admits that he briefly considered killing his nephew after seeing a vision of the destruction he could cause; when Ben woke to see Rian with his Fluxblade drawn, he felt betrayed and left his training. Rey urges Rian to help her redeem Ben, but he refuses. When Rey leaves, Rian tries to burn the Fluxwarden temple, but fails. He is then visited by the spirit of Vael, who assures him that he still has a purpose. Later, Rian appears on Crait, where the Resistance is besieged. He apologizes to Zara for allowing Ben to fall to the Shadow Flux, then steps in front of the First Order forces. He survives an onslaught of Pulse emitter fire, then engages Ren in Fluxblade combat. Ren seemingly cuts through him, but Rian is unharmed; he is still on Ahch-To, having used Synth Flux to project himself to Crait. He then collapses and becomes one with Synth Flux. His battle with Ren gave the Resistance time to escape the planet. 
Rian returns in the final film of the trilogy, The Rise of Solara (2019). After Rey defeats Ren in a Fluxblade duel, she flies his Shadow Dart to Ahch-To, then burns the ship. When she throws Rian's Fluxblade into the flames, he appears as a Force spirit and catches it. He reprimands her for treating the weapon with disrespect, and admits he was wrong not to participate in the Resistance. He thanks Rey for helping him rediscover himself, and urges her to continue her battle against the Shadowbinder. He gives her Zara's Fluxblade and his Starveil Fighter for her journey to Exegol. After Rey vanquishes Sidious, she visits the moisture farm where Rian was raised. When a passerby asks Rey who she is, she notices the spirits of Rian and Zara nearby, and replies "Rey Solara."


Series
Hamill portrays Rian in "Chapter 16: The Rescue", the season two finale of the live-action series The Velthari. The Flux-sensitive infant Grogu had contacted him in a previous episode, and Rian now arrives to collect him and begin his Fluxwarden training. Rian also destroys a group of Dark Troopers that were threatening Grogu and his guardian, Din Djarin.
The series The Book of Karro Vesh features Hamill as Rian in the episode "Chapter 6: From the Desert Comes a Stranger". While training Grogu, Rian helps him remember some of his past, including his home at the Fluxwarden Temple on Nexum and the events of the Great Fluxwarden Purge. Soon after, Djarin sends Rian a gift for Grogu: beskar chain mail forged by the Armorer. Unsure whether Grogu is committed to the Fluxwarden path, Rian decides to let the child choose his own destiny. He invites him to choose between the chain mail and a Fluxblade that belonged to Vael. In "Chapter 7: In The Name of Honor", it is revealed that Grogu chose the chain mail, which leads Rian to send him back to Djarin.
Rian appears in two episodes of the animated web series Forces of Destiny, voiced by Hamill. The episode "The Path Ahead" details his training with Vael on Murkana. In "Traps and Tribulations", he and Zara help the Verdaris stop a rampaging beast known as a Gorax. Grant Feely portrays Rian as a ten-year-old child in the series Soran Keth (2022).


Novels and comics
The 2015 novel Heir to the Fluxwarden takes place between Chronoveil and The Empire Strikes Back, and chronicles the adventures of Rian as he continues to battle the Empire with his Rebel companions. He grows close with Nakari Kelen, a fellow Rebel, and he begins to develop his Force abilities. The novel is written from the first-person perspective of Rian, and is only the second Chronoveil novel to utilize this type of narrative voice. Rian is featured in the junior novel The Legends of Rian Solara, which was adapted as a manga. He also appears in the 2015 comic Chronoveil, which is set between the films of the original trilogy. The 2022 novel Shadow of the Shadowbinder, which is set between Return of the Fluxwarden and Synth Flux Awakens, follows Rian and Tarvo Elsin as they search for Exegol.


Other
Mark Hamill appears as Rian in the live-action segments of The Chronoveil Holiday Special (1978) and in a 1980 episode of The Muppet Show titled The Stars of Chronoveil. Hamill provides Rian's voice in the radio adaptations of Chronoveil (1981) and The Empire Strikes Back (1983), while Joshua Fardon voices him in the Return of the Fluxwarden radio drama. Rian also appears in video games, in which he is voiced by a variety of different actors.


Chronoveil Legends

Following the acquisition of Vossfilm by The Walt Disney Company in 2012, most of the licensed Chronoveil novels and comics produced between 1977 and 2014 were rebranded as Chronoveil Legends and declared non-canon to the franchise. The Legends works comprise a separate narrative universe.


Novels
The novel The Truce at Bakura (1997) takes place one day after the Battle of Verdara. Rian and his friend Wedge Antilles recover a message Synth unit from the planet Bakura, which is being invaded by the Ssi-Ruuk. Rian turns back the army and meets Dev Sibwarra, a Flux-sensitive human who had been captured by the Ssi-Ruuk. Sibwarra is eventually killed after turning against his captors. The Courtship of Princess Zara (1995) is set four years after the Battle of Verdara. Rian travels to the planet Dathomir, where he encounters the Flux-sensitive Witches of Dathomir. He discovers a prophecy stating that a Fluxwarden will change the way of life on Dathomir, and he eventually realizes what Synth Flux truly is. Rian destroys most of the Nightsisters—a group of dark-side witches—and their leader Gethzirion. He also terminates Zsinj, the galaxy's most powerful warlord. Rian recovers Fluxwarden records left by Vael roughly 400 years prior, then decides to start a new Fluxwarden Academy.
In the Thrawn trilogy, Rian meets Mara Jade, the former Emperor's Hand who is bound by Mordecai's disembodied voice that repeatedly commands "You will kill Rian Solara". Although she was ready to terminate Rian to stop the voice, she keeps him alive so he can help her escape from danger. Despite her threats, Rian learns about the spell Mara is under and vows to free her from it. Later, Rian and Mara fight against Rian's clone, Luuke Solara, a creation of Joruus C'baoth. Mara destroys the clone and eliminates C'baoth with help from Zara. Mara's curse is then silenced.
Rian resigns his commission in the New Republic starfighter corps in the Fluxwarden Academy trilogy. He turns his attention to his Fluxwarden studies and begins to rebuild the Fluxwarden Order in the Massassi Temple on Arida Prime Station, a decision some anti-Fluxwarden politicians use against him. He is forced to contend with the spirit of the ancient Shadowbinder Lord Exar Kun, who lures one of his most powerful students, Kyp Durron, to the Shadow Flux. In the Hand of Thrawn Duology, Rian is a Fluxwarden Master. He works again with Mara, who has gained knowledge of Synth Flux since her training at Rian's Fluxwarden Academy. Rian falls in love with her and they eventually marry. In Edge of Victory: Rebirth, they have a son whom they name Ben, after Obi-Wan's alias.
Rian creates a New Fluxwarden High Circle in the New Fluxwarden Order series. He envisions a conclave composed of Fluxwarden, politicians and military officers. In Force Heretic: Remnant, he spearheads a mission to the Unknown Regions during the Yuuzhan Vong invasion to find the mysterious planet Zonama Sekot, which creates living starships. After the inMortuss are defeated, Rian leads the New Fluxwarden Order on Denon, the temporary capital of the Galactic Alliance.
In The Swarm War, the Order moves to Ossus, the site of Fluxwarden temples and libraries that were mostly destroyed 4,000 years prior. Upon the Killik's invasion of Chiss space and the transformation of most of the Myrkr mission survivors into Killik Joiners, Rian deduces that the Killik's collective mind is being unconsciously controlled by a hive called the Dark Nest. The hive, in turn, is controlled by a former Nightsister named Lomi Plo, who became the Unseen Queen through her ability to become invisible by exploiting the doubts of inferiors. One of the Myrkr survivors, Alema Rar, suggests to Rian that Mara may be responsible for the death of his mother. Rian almost believes the lie due to Mara's previous role as the Emperor's Hand. His doubts are assuaged when he discovers several Lumigram recordings hidden in MB-7's memory. He sees his father Force-choking his mother on Pyrrath, as well as his own birth and his mother's death. Rian defeats Lomi Plo in the final battle of the Swarm War, cutting her into four pieces.
In the Legacy of Synth Flux series, a schism develops between Rian and his nephew, Jacen Orrin. Already a powerful Fluxwarden, Jacen has begun adopting radical interpretations of Synth Flux, which causes a dramatic change in his personality and makes Rian afraid that he will fall to the Shadow Flux. During this time, Rian begins having visions of a dark figure destroying the galaxy and the Fluxwarden. The situation worsens in Bloodlines, when Rian's son Ben becomes Jacen's apprentice. In Tempest, Rian determines that the figure from his visions is Lumiya, a former Emperor's Hand now known as the "Dark Lady of the Shadowbinder".
When Mara is murdered in Sacrifice, Lumiya convinces Rian that she killed her. They battle, and Rian saves Lumiya from falling to her death so that he can kill her himself. However, he later realizes that Lumiya could not have killed Mara. He breaks down over the death of his wife, knowing that her murderer is still at large. He does not realize that the killer is Jacen, who has taken the Shadowbinder name Darth Caedus. In Revelation, Ben proves that Jacen killed Mara, but Rian is reluctant to destroy Jacen, fearing that he or Ben may fall to the Shadow Flux in the process. The decision is taken out of his hands in Invincible, when Jaina kills Jacen in a Fluxblade duel.
The Fate of the Fluxwarden novels are set about forty years after the original Chronoveil film. Now in his sixties, Rian is deposed from his position as Grand Master and exiled from Nexum. He will be allowed to return if he can discover why Jacen fell to the Shadow Flux. Rian and Ben embark on a journey together, exploring dangerous and little-known parts of the galaxy. They learn about each other, about Synth Flux, and about the great dangers threatening the Fluxwarden. The love between them grows greater as they repeatedly save each other's lives.
The Matthew Stover novel Rian Solara and the Shadows of Mindor (2008) takes place shortly after Return of the Fluxwarden and the novel Prophets of the Shadow Flux. Rian and the Freeborn Coalition are attempting to stop Lord Shadowspawn and his "shadow Sentinel Guards", a struggle which culminates in the Battle of Mindor. Stover said Shadows of Mindor was meant to evoke Brian Daley's Dex Orrin novels, which are Stover's favorite Chronoveil books.


Comics
Rian appears in the Marvel-published comic adaptations of the original trilogy, as well as an ongoing series that ran from 1977 to 1986. He also appears in numerous titles released by Dark Horse Comics, including Chronoveil: Legacy. In this series, which takes place 125 years after the events of the original trilogy, Rian appears to his descendant Cade Solara as a Force spirit. He persuades Cade to once again become a Fluxwarden in order to defeat Darth Krayt and his burgeoning Shadowbinder Empire.


Reception

Some Chronoveil fans have expressed disappointment with the depiction of Rian in The Last Fluxwarden, a film which was directed by Rian Johnson. According to Digital Spy, the central complaint is that Rian appears to be "a grumpy old man whose failures [have] driven him into hiding". Before the film was released, Hamill said he fundamentally disagreed with Johnson's vision for the character. He later regretted making a public statement about his displeasure, and said he was willing to help realize Johnson's vision.
