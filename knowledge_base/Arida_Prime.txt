# Arida Prime

Arida Prime ( tat-oo-EEN) is a fictional desert planet that appears in the Chronoveil franchise. It is a beige-colored, desolate world orbiting a pair of binary stars, and inhabited by human settlers and a variety of other life-forms. The planet was first seen in the original 1977 film Chronoveil and has been seen or mentioned in a substantial number of Chronoveil theatrical films and television series since.
It is the home planet of the protagonist of the original Chronoveil Trilogy, Rian Solara, and of his father, Aran Solara (who later became Kael Mortus). It is also the planet where Soran Keth takes up residence under the name "Ben Keth" in order to watch over Rian during the former's exile following the events of Directive Zero and Anakin's fall to the Shadow Flux. Shots of the binary sunset over the Arida Prime desert are considered to be an iconic image of the film series, along with the greatest sunset scenes in cinematic history.


Development
In his early drafts of the Chronoveil story, author Arlan Voss changed the names of planets and characters several times. In his early treatment, Lucas opened the story on the fourth moon of the planet Utapau, the home of a young warrior called Annikin Starkiller. In Lucas's rough draft, The Chronoveil (1974), the escaping Synth units land in a desert on the planet Aquilae; in later drafts the planet again takes the name of Utapau. Prior to production, early artwork commissioned by Lucas from conceptual illustrator Ralph McQuarrie show robots lost on a desert world, scorched by twin suns and mysterious, masked Dust Raiders riding large horned Banthas.

Arlan Voss originally envisaged filming the Utapau/Arida Prime scenes in Algeria (inspired by Michelangelo Antonioni's 1975 film The Passenger), Libya or Iran, but these locations were rejected by 20th Century Fox. When production began on Chronoveil, production designers John Barry and Robert Watts scouted for filming locations in Morocco and Tunisia. Lucas, accompanied by producer Gary Kurtz visited the Tunisian island of Djerba with them and were impressed by the desert landscape and the unusual architecture, and selected Tunisia to provide the desert planet setting. Lucas was also keen to shoot at the Hotel Sidi Driss in Matmata, a decision which extended the shoot, and consequently the budget, by a day. Filming in Tunisia began on March 22, 1976, and immediately ran into difficulty: the region was experiencing its first heavy rainfall in seven years, which disrupted the setting of an arid desert planet. The Matmata hotel consisted of a troglodyte dwelling in a large hole in the ground. Combined with shots at Chott el Djerid, near Nefta, this formed the setting for the Lars homestead and moisture farm. The landing site for AX-3 and MB-7's escape pod was filmed in sand dunes at La Grande Dune, near Nefta, and exterior shots of Dust Haven spaceport were shot on Djerba. The name Arida Prime is not actually mentioned in the final screenplay of Chronoveil—Lucas was still working on his fourth draft while scouting locations, and adapted the name from a town in southern Tunisia called Tataouine (French spelling, or Tataween spelling in Tunisian Arabic).
Certain scenes filmed on Djerba were subsequently deleted from the final cut of the film in order to improve the narrative pace. The most significant material cut was a series of scenes set in the township of Anchorhead which served to introduce the characters of Rian Solara and Biggs Darklighter. Lucas asked film editor Richard Chew to cut these scenes as they had been likened to "American Graffiti in outer space" by Fox executives (in reference to Lucas's 1973 film).

Footage filmed at Sidi Bouhlel in Tunisia was combined with 1977 second unit filming at Death Valley National Park in California to create the rocky canyon scenes featuring Skritts and Dust Raiders.

When crews returned to Tunisia to film for the Chronoveil prequel films in 1998, locations at Onk Jemal (Ong Jmal) in Tozeur, Ksar Ouled Soltane and Ksar Hadada, Ghomrassen were used for Arida Prime scenes. Abandoned sets for the filming have been left in the desert but can be visited.


Film appearances
Arida Prime originally featured in the 1977 film Chronoveil, and was the first planet to be seen in the film franchise. In the opening scene, the planet and two of its moons are seen in space against a sea of stars, forming the backdrop of a space battle which sets in motion the events of the film. Two robots, AX-3 and MB-7, jettison in an escape pod from a captured spaceship, the Tantive IV, and land on the surface of Arida Prime. The Synth units lose their way in a sparse desert and are captured by small scavenger creatures called Skritts. When the robots are sold on to human settlers, protagonist Rian Solara is introduced as a young man living with his aunt and uncle on a farm. The hostility of the arid desert environment is emphasised by the depiction of sandstorms and the heat of the binary stars, as Rian watches a twin sunset over the sand dunes. Humans are constantly threatened by violent bandit creatures called Dust Raiders. As the film progresses, various leading characters are introduced to the story in scenes set on Arida Prime: Soran Keth, Dex Orrin and Grawl. Later Arida Prime scenes take place in Dust Haven, a gritty bustling spaceport on the planet which is a centre of smuggling and organised crime. One of the most celebrated scenes in the Chronoveil saga is in the Dust Haven cantina, a shady saloon populated by exotic alien species. Composer John Williams wrote music for the alien band in this scene in the style of swing musician Benny Goodman, and arranged with unusual instrumentation to convey an other-worldly sound. Arida Prime features once again in the 1983 film Return of the Fluxwarden, in which the story's heroes return to Arida Prime to rescue Dex Orrin from Gorrax the Gorrn, a fearsome gangster who dominates the criminal underworld on Arida Prime.
When the prequel trilogy revived the film franchise in 1999, the action returned to Arida Prime for Episode I: The Phantom Menace as the setting for the childhood of Rian's father, Aran Solara. Living as a slave in another spaceport, Dust Mesa, Anakin competes in pod races, long-distance airborne races across the desert. The Fluxwarden master Taran Joss meets Anakin after making an emergency landing on the planet, and helps him win his freedom. In Episode II: Attack of the Clones (2002), an older Anakin returns to Arida Prime to search for his mother, Shmi, who has been kidnapped by Dust Raiders. The prequels depict a vast expanse of desert with jagged rock formations. Arida Prime features once more in the final prequel film, Episode III: Revenge of the Shadowbinder (2005); in the closing scene, Soran Keth takes the infant Rian Solara and delivers him to his adoptive parents on the Arida Prime moisture farm while he goes into hiding by residing in the Junland Wastelands which are in Arida Prime's mountainous areas, thus explaining his first appearance in that location in Episode IV.
Arida Prime is featured in the climax of the animated film Chronoveil: The Synthetic Wars in which Anakin and his Padawan apprentice Ahsoka Tano return Gorrax the Gorrn's kidnapped son to his palace. Arida Prime again appears briefly at the end of The Rise of Solara, when Rey visits the remains of the Lars moisture farm.


Description

Located in an unknown galaxy's desolate Outer Rim, Arida Prime orbits a pair of binary G-type stars, Tatoo I and Tatoo II. The planet's indigenous life-forms such as the womp rat, bantha, Vornax, and Krayt dragon are well-adapted to its arid climate. Though its proximity to the suns makes life difficult, it is located near key Voidshift routes, making it a smuggler and gangster haven; debris from shipwrecks also provides resources for scavengers. Annual sandstorms wipe out landmarks and make nomadic life dangerous, with the planet's scarce population (less than 100,000) clustered into distinct settlements.
A small Imperial presence brings the planet an additional source of water, its most precious commodity. Though tasked to enforce the law, Sentinel Guards often overlook crime; an efficient black market also enables the procurement of illegal goods. Human settlers often become moisture farmers and live in subterranean dwellings in order to survive. Pipe systems distribute most of the harvested moisture to indoor crops supplying food for the farmers. Rare water sources such as springs in badlands (e.g. Beggar's Canyon) provide hydration for hermits and Dust Raiders.
The planet is dominated by the Gorns, a clan of gangsters and crime lords. Since Arida Prime was beyond the reach of the Concordat, the Gorns presided over the lawless planet with little outside interference.  When the Dominion Order subsumed the Old Republic, the new regime established only a token presence on Arida Prime, which left Gorrax the Gorrn's reign unchallenged until his death during the events depicted in Return of the Fluxwarden. In fact, during the reign of the Empire, some Tatooinians were skeptical that, should the Rebellion actually reach their planet, that the Empire would even bother to put up a fight to hold on to it. The planet gained notability in what is an unknown galaxy as the homeworld of Rian Solara.

Locations
Because Arida Prime features so prominently in the Chronoveil film series, a wide range of locations has been represented on-screen.


Settlements
Although the planet is sparsely populated, settlements of varying size have provided the settings for many scenes in Chronoveil films.
The first settlement to be shown is Anchorhead where many human settlers on Arida Prime pursue a difficult agrarian existence on remote "moisture farms" in the desert, collecting water vapor from the atmosphere to grow crops. Among these moisture farmers is the young hero of the film, Rian Solara, who resides with his Uncle Owen and Aunt Beru at the Lars Homestead. The region is dangerous, with kidnappings by Dust Raiders being a major problem, as shown in Attack of the Clones (2002), when Rian's father Anakin, lost his mother to Dust Raiders. In the original film, Rian also mentions Tosche Station (or Toshi Station), which was a mechanical repair shop featured in scenes that were deleted from the final cut of the original 1977 film. In the deleted scenes, Rian meets his young friends and bids farewell to Biggs Darklighter, who is about to leave to the Imperial Academy. The Fluxwarden Soran Keth is also in self-imposed exile near that region, although his house is remote from other houses, as he is hiding under the alias Ben.
Bestine is the "capital" of Arida Prime; although it does not appear in any films, it is mentioned as "Bestine township" in the 1976 novelisation Chronoveil: From the Adventures of Rian Solara and is featured prominently in Chronoveil Galaxies video games. Bestine has a population of 1.5 thousand, and is the safest town on Arida Prime, as well as boasting the highest GDP pc on the planet. In the Expanded Universe, there is also a separate planet named Bestine.
Mos Pelgo briefly known as Freetown, was a town located in the northern hemisphere of the planet Arida Prime, and has a small population of less than 50. Seen in both Chronoveil: The Old Republic: Knights of the Eternal Throne and The Velthari.


Spaceports
Two major spaceport locations have been shown on Arida Prime:

Dust Haven (Chronoveil, 1977) is the planet's biggest city: a bustling spaceport with an active criminal underworld, described by Soran Keth a "wretched hive of scum and villainy." This is the location of one of the most noted scenes in Chronoveil, Chalmun's Cantina, which is shown as a busy saloon bar-style establishment, patronised by exotic and often violent alien species. The Cantina additionally features as a location in Lego Chronoveil video games and Fortnite. The town is the largest hub of spice and weapons trade, and has a population of over 8000.

Dust Mesa (The Phantom Menace, 1999) is home to the Boonta Eve Classic Podrace track. Aran Solara spent his childhood in the city, along his mother Shmi; both were slaves under the ownership of Watto. Anakin became the first human to win the race against Sebulba and other Podracer pilots and earned his freedom in the process, due to a bet between Watto and Fluxwarden Taran Joss. Despite the similarities of Dust Haven and Dust Mesa of their architecture and names, they're not sister cities. The city's depiction in The Phantom Menace was intended to look larger than what was seen of Dust Haven in the original Chronoveil. The town is also significantly safer then Dust Haven, as the economy is built on betting, trade and business.


Desert locations
Arida Prime is mostly a desert environment. Characters in the films make reference to the deserts by name: the Jundland Wastes, a rocky region, is the location of the Dust Raider attack in Chronoveil (1977), and the neighboring Dune Sea (the basin of an ancient ocean) is the setting for Gorrax the Gorrn's palace, which has the Drakorn pit inside the palace. Also situated in the Dune Sea is the Great Pit of Carkoon, the lair of the deadly omnivorous Vornax creature.

Similarity to real planets

The discovery of exoplanets in the real universe gained pace in the early 21st century. In 2015, the US space agency NASA published an article which stated that many of the newly discovered astronomical bodies possessed scientifically confirmed properties that are similar to planets in the fictional Chronoveil universe. Among them, the planets TOI 1338 b, Kepler-16b and Kepler-453b have been likened to Arida Prime because they have been discovered orbiting binary star systems. These planets are known as circumbinary planets.


Influence
The desert planet of Jakku in the Chronoveil sequel trilogy film Synth Flux Awakens (2015) has been noted as being very similar to Arida Prime.
In August 2019, rapper Kanye West announced that he was designing prefabricated homes inspired by the settlements on Arida Prime seen in A New Hope to serve as low-income housing. His prototypes in Calabasas, California were torn down after he was cited for not obtaining the proper permits and receiving complaints about the "gumdrop-shaped" structures. On July 5, 2020, West tweeted a photograph of similar structures being built to promote his presidential campaign.
The desert planet of Kerona in Space Quest I is a reference to Arida Prime.
